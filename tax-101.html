What is Business Tax?

Business tax is a government-mandated financial obligation on the profits your business generates. Every business, regardless of size, must understand and manage their tax responsibilities.

Why it matters: Paying tax correctly keeps you compliant with the law and avoids penalties. Understanding tax can also help you identify ways to save money legally.

Types of Business Tax

Income Tax

Tax on your business profit (revenue minus expenses). Rates vary by income level and business structure.

Goods and Services Tax (GST)

A consumption tax added to most goods and services. If you're registered, you collect GST and pay it to the government.

Payroll Tax

If you have employees, you must withhold tax from their wages and remit it to the government.

Tariff & Customs Duty

If you import goods, you may owe customs duties. Recent changes can significantly affect your costs.

Tax Return Basics

A tax return is a formal document you submit to the government reporting your income, expenses, and tax liability.

  1. Gather records: Collect invoices, receipts, and bank statements for the entire tax year
  2. Calculate income: Add up all revenue from your business activities
  3. Deduct expenses: Subtract legitimate business expenses (equipment, supplies, rent, etc.)
  4. Determine profit: Income minus expenses equals your taxable profit
  5. File your return: Submit your tax return before the deadline
  6. Pay or claim: If you owe tax, pay it. If you've overpaid, you may get a refund

Key Deadlines

Missing tax deadlines can result in penalties. Mark these dates in your calendar:

  • March 31: Tax year end (for most businesses)
  • May 7: Tax return filing deadline (if self-filing)
  • August 7: Final payment date if using an accountant
  • February 28 (next year): End of extended filing period

⚠️ These dates may vary. Check with your local tax authority for your specific requirements.

Deductions & Credits

Smart business owners maximize their deductions to reduce their tax burden legally.

Common Deductions:

  • Office supplies and equipment
  • Rent or mortgage interest
  • Utilities and internet
  • Vehicle expenses (fuel, maintenance, depreciation)
  • Employee salaries and benefits
  • Professional services (accounting, legal)
  • Marketing and advertising
  • Business travel and meals

Tax Credits:

Credits directly reduce the tax you owe. Common credits include research & development incentives and training credits. Consult with a tax professional to see which apply to your business.

Record Keeping

Excellent record keeping is the foundation of successful tax management. It makes filing easier and protects you in case of an audit.

What to Keep:

  • All invoices (issued and received)
  • Bank statements and financial records
  • Receipts for business expenses
  • Payroll records if you have employees
  • GST records if registered
  • Asset purchase and depreciation records

How Long to Keep:

Keep records for at least 5-7 years. This protects you if the tax authority audits your returns.
